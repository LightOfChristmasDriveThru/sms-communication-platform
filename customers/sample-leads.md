# Sample Lead Data for Demo

Use these realistic lead scenarios when building the demo flow.

## Lead #1: Fall Risk Care

**Channel**: Phone call (transcribed by Twilio)

> "Hi, I'm calling about in-home care for my mother. She's 78, had a fall last month, and our family is worried about her living alone. She needs someone during the day while I'm at work. Can you help?"

**Context**: 
- Came in at 5:47pm on a Tuesday
- Caller is an adult child, not the client
- Time-sensitive (worried about safety)
- High-intent (already experienced a fall)

**Suggested Reply**:
> "Hi there! Thanks for calling. We specialize in fall-risk care and support for seniors living independently. We can absolutely help your mom feel safer at home. Let's schedule a quick intake call tomorrow morning to understand her needs and get care started. Does 10am work for you?"

## Lead #2: Cognitive Decline Support

**Channel**: Web form submission

**Form data**:
- Name: Sarah Chen
- Phone: (555) 234-5678
- Message: "My father is in early-stage dementia. He's still independent but forgets appointments and needs reminders. Do you offer memory support services?"

**Context**:
- Submitted at 4:22pm on a Thursday
- Adult child seeking specialized care
- Looking for ongoing support, not emergency
- High-touch situation

**Suggested Reply**:
> "Hi Sarah, thank you for reaching out about your father's care. We have experience with memory support and daily reminders for seniors with cognitive changes. Let's schedule a time this week to talk through what would help him most. I'm available tomorrow at 2pm or Thursday at 10am—which works better?"

---

## Lead #3: Post-Discharge Care

**Channel**: Email

**Subject**: "Post-op care for my husband"

**Body**:
> "My husband is having hip surgery next month and will need help at home during recovery. He's 72. We're looking for someone to help with physical therapy follow-ups and daily tasks. Do you cover post-surgical care?"

**Context**:
- Sent at 3:15pm (within business hours, but near end of day)
- Scheduled care (predictable start date)
- Medical coordination required
- Medium-length engagement (6-12 weeks)

**Suggested Reply**:
> "Hi [name], congratulations on getting him the care he needs. We absolutely work with post-surgical recovery—physical therapy coordination, mobility assistance, and daily support are exactly what we do. Let's set up a 15-min call to go over his surgery date and needs. When can we talk this week?"

---

## Lead #4: Overnight Care (High Urgency)

**Channel**: Phone call

**Transcription**:
> "I'm calling because my mom fell during the night and can't be alone anymore. She's in the hospital now, but she'll be discharged in two days. We need 24-hour care immediately. Can you help?"

**Context**:
- Called at 8:47am (urgent)
- Crisis situation (hospital discharge)
- High-need client (24-hr care required)
- Immediate timeline (2 days)

**Suggested Reply**:
> "I'm so glad she's getting care. We can absolutely support her 24-hour care needs when she comes home. This is exactly our specialty. I'm going to get you connected with our care coordinator right now so we can assess her needs and start care immediately. Can you give me the hospital's discharge planner's info?"

## Common Homecare Language to Use

**Things homecare owners say:**
- "Lead came in" (not "conversion opportunity" or "prospective client")
- "Intake call" (the first assessment call with a potential client)
- "Care starts" or "admit" (when a client begins using their services)
- "Visit" (a single care session)
- "Caregiver" (their staff)
- "Day shift" / "night shift"
- "Fall risk," "memory care," "mobility," "ADLs" (activities of daily living)
- "Discharge planner" (hospital staff who refer clients)
- "Care plan" (the client's care protocol)

**Avoid:**
- "Patient" (they say "client")
- "Platform" or "software"
- "Workflow" or "integration"
- "Leverage," "optimize," "seamless"

---

## Notes for Demo Building

1. **Use real language**: These are real lead types and real concerns. Use them as-is in the demo.
2. **Show urgency**: Different leads have different urgency levels. A fall-risk lead (Lead #1) and overnight care lead (Lead #4) need faster responses than post-discharge care.
3. **Suggested replies matter**: The reply should be specific to the lead type and caring in tone, not robotic.
4. **Response time is the hero**: Show that replying within 30 minutes (not 24 hours) is what matters.
